# Planning Document Types

Before converting a skill into a business (see [Skill vs. Business Literacy](skill-vs-business-literacy.md)), it helps to know that "make a plan" isn't one document — it's several distinct planning artifacts, each answering a different question.

## The Core Documents

| Document | Answers | Typical scope |
|---|---|---|
| **Market research** | Who are the customers, competitors, and what does the market actually look like? | Done first, feeds everything else |
| **Business plan** | Is this venture viable — model, costs, revenue, funding? | Whole-business, often investor-facing |
| **Strategic plan** | Where is the business going over the next 1-3+ years, and why? | High-level direction and priorities |
| **Marketing plan** | How will the target audience be reached and converted? | Channels, budget, positioning |
| **Content plan** | What content gets published, where, and on what cadence? | Execution detail under the marketing plan |
| **Action plan** | Who does what, by when, to execute the above? | Task-level, short time horizon |

Each layer gets more concrete than the one above it — market research and the business plan set the "why," the strategic and marketing plans set the "what," and the content and action plans set the "how, specifically, this week."

## Why the Distinction Matters

A founder who says "I made a plan" after writing a single page of goals has usually only sketched a strategic plan, and skipped market research, a real marketing plan, and any action-level detail — which is why the "plan" doesn't survive contact with execution. Knowing these are separate documents makes it obvious which one is missing when a business feels directionless despite having "a plan."

## You Don't Need to Master Every Document to Start

The realistic path isn't formal training in each of these (an MBA-level treatment of strategic planning is its own multi-year discipline) — it's reading broadly enough across all of them to recognize what each one should contain, so that when a specialist or agency is hired to produce one, there's enough literacy to brief them properly and evaluate what comes back, rather than accepting anything confidently presented.

## Common Questions

**Which of these should a solo founder write themselves vs. delegate?**
> Market research and the business plan are worth doing personally even roughly, since they force clarity on the venture itself. Marketing and content plans are commonly delegated to a specialist or agency once there's budget — but only once the founder has enough literacy in the topic (see [Skill vs. Business Literacy](skill-vs-business-literacy.md)) to brief and evaluate that specialist.

**Does a small business really need all six documents?**
> The *rigor* scales with the business, not the existence of each layer — a small business's "market research" might be a week of talking to potential customers rather than a formal study, but skipping the layer entirely (going straight from an idea to an action plan) is what causes expensive, avoidable mistakes.
