# Owner Compensation & Fund Separation

One of the most common causes of a founder personally struggling despite the business "doing fine": treating company money and personal money as one undivided pool, instead of paying yourself a defined salary like any other employee.

## The Failure Pattern

A founder who doesn't take a regular salary either under-spends personally while the business sits on cash, or dips into company funds ad hoc whenever personal cash is needed — both create the same problem: nobody, including the founder, can tell whether the *business* is actually profitable, because business and personal cash flows are entangled. At year-end, the founder looks at whatever's left and calls it "profit," having effectively worked unpaid all year in exchange for an uncertain year-end number.

## The Fix: Pay Yourself Like an Employee

Set a defined, regular owner's salary — decided deliberately, not as leftover cash — and treat withdrawals beyond that salary the same way any other profit distribution would be treated (see [Profit Reinvestment Discipline](profit-reinvestment-discipline.md) for how much of that remainder should stay in the business). This does two things:

1. **Makes the business's real profitability visible** — once owner compensation is a fixed line-item cost like any employee's salary, the remaining number is an honest profit figure, comparable across periods.
2. **Reduces the temptation to raid the business informally** — a founder with a defined salary has less reason to make ad hoc withdrawals that make cash flow harder to forecast.

## Why This Especially Matters for Solo Founders

An employee working under a founder typically has fixed hours; a founder who's actually running the business is very often working *more* hours than their own employees, while being the one most likely to skip paying themselves properly. The reminder here is direct: don't treat yourself as exempt from a fair wage just because you own the business — the labor is still real and should be compensated as such, not deferred indefinitely to "whatever's left."

## Common Questions

**Isn't taking a fixed salary risky if the business has an unpredictable revenue month?**
> This is exactly why it should be planned alongside [cash flow management](cash-flow-management.md) — a sustainable owner salary is set at a level the business can support through its weaker months too, not its best month.

**Does this apply to co-founders as well as solo founders?**
> Yes, and arguably more so — undefined compensation between co-founders (versus employees, who have clear contracts) is a common source of resentment and conflict; see [Technical/Business Co-Founder Partnership](../../business/concepts/entrepreneurship/technical-business-cofounder-partnership.md) for the broader partnership-structure issue this connects to.
