# Profit Reinvestment Discipline

A recurring rule from experienced operators: don't pocket every unit of profit as it comes in. Deliberately hold back a portion for reinvestment — R&D, new products, marketing experiments — even when the business is doing well and there's no immediate emergency forcing that discipline.

## Why This Is Easy to Skip

When a business is profitable, there's no obvious pain signal telling the owner to reinvest rather than withdraw — profit *feels* like a reward for the current model working, not a reminder that the current model has a shelf life. The businesses that keep growing are usually the ones that treat a reinvestment fund as non-negotiable, the same way payroll or rent is non-negotiable, rather than as a nice-to-have that only happens with "leftover" money.

## What Counts as Reinvestment

- **R&D / product development** — improving or expanding what's actually being sold
- **New product or category testing** — entering an adjacent [niche](../../marketing/concepts/fundamentals/niche-specialization.md) once the current one is proven
- **Marketing experiments** — testing new channels or creative approaches beyond the proven baseline (see [Creative Testing & Scaling](../../marketing/concepts/digital-marketing/creative-testing-scaling.md))
- **Operational capacity** — team, tools, or infrastructure that removes a current bottleneck before it becomes urgent

## The Greed Trap

Taking maximum profit out of the business every period, with no reinvestment buffer, is described as a form of greed that eventually catches up with a business — competitors who *are* reinvesting will out-innovate and out-market a business that's optimizing for immediate withdrawal. This isn't an argument for never taking profit; it's an argument for treating some fixed reinvestment share as an operating cost, not a discretionary choice made after the fact.

## Common Questions

**How much should be reinvested vs. taken as profit?**
> There's no universal percentage — the point is having *a* deliberate, consistent rule (even a modest one) rather than deciding case-by-case after seeing how good a period was, which tends to drift toward taking everything during good periods.

**How does this relate to owner compensation?**
> This is about company-level reinvestment; [separating owner salary from company profit](owner-compensation-and-fund-separation.md) is a related but distinct discipline — both are about not treating "whatever's left in the account" as a single undifferentiated pool of spendable money.
