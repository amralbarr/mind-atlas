# Hiring PR / Creator-Outreach Staff

The role responsible for sourcing and negotiating with influencers, UGC creators, and bloggers (see [Content Creator Types](../../../marketing/concepts/digital-marketing/content-creator-types.md)) has a specific, easy-to-misjudge skill profile — and a common hiring mistake is optimizing for the wrong trait entirely.

## The Wrong Assumption

A frequent misconception is that this role is primarily about presentation — hiring someone who "looks good" or is personable on camera. In practice, the role rarely involves meeting anyone in person; it's a high-volume communication and negotiation job conducted mostly over messaging and calls.

## The Actual Required Skills

| Skill | Why it's required |
|---|---|
| **Strong communication** | Constantly pitching, explaining briefs, and following up with creators over chat/phone |
| **Negotiation** | Getting a fair rate from a creator/influencer without overpaying or damaging the relationship |
| **Multitasking** | Managing outreach to dozens of creators simultaneously, each at a different stage of the pipeline |
| **Organization / follow-through** | Tracking who owes what content, by when, without things falling through the cracks |

A candidate missing these — regardless of how polished or personable they present in an interview — will struggle in the role, because the day-to-day work is high-volume coordination, not in-person representation.

## Why This Role Needs to Exist In-House

Outsourcing creator relationships entirely to an external agency without any internal owner means nobody internally has the direct relationships, negotiated rates, or pipeline visibility that compounds over time — see [In-House Marketing Team vs. Agency](../../../management/concepts/leadership/in-house-marketing-team-vs-agency.md) for the broader principle that scaling spend requires *someone* internally who owns and understands that piece of the operation, whether or not execution itself is outsourced.

## Common Questions

**Should this role be combined with a broader marketing/content role, or kept separate?**
> At smaller scale, it's often combined with content or social media management; once creator outreach volume grows (many creators, frequent campaigns), it becomes a full-time coordination job in its own right, distinct from content strategy or creative direction.

**How do you evaluate negotiation skill in an interview?**
> Role-playing a realistic negotiation scenario (agreeing a rate with a difficult, self-important creator) tends to reveal far more than asking a candidate to describe their negotiation skills in the abstract.
