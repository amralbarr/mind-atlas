# SEO Fundamentals

Search Engine Optimization: improving a site's visibility in unpaid ("organic") search results. The goal is to rank for the terms a target audience actually searches when they have the problem the product solves.

## The Three Pillars

| Pillar | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **[Technical SEO](../../../engineering/concepts/frontend/technical-seo.md)** | Crawlability, site speed, mobile-friendliness, structured data, indexability |
| **On-page SEO** | Content quality, keyword targeting, title tags, headers, internal linking |
| **Off-page SEO** | Backlinks from other sites, brand mentions, domain authority |

A page can be perfectly written and still rank poorly if the site is technically broken (slow, not indexed, no backlinks) — all three pillars have to hold at once.

## Keyword Intent

Not all keywords deserve the same content, because searchers behind them want different things:

| Intent | Example query | What should rank |
|---|---|---|
| **Informational** | "what is a [value proposition](../fundamentals/value-proposition.md)" | A blog post/guide |
| **Navigational** | "hubspot login" | The brand's own site |
| **Commercial investigation** | "best CRM for small business" | A comparison page |
| **Transactional** | "buy CRM software" | A pricing/signup page |

Targeting the wrong intent (e.g. a hard sales page for an informational query) tends to rank poorly and convert poorly even if it ranks.

## On-Page Basics

- **Title tag & meta description** — the clickable headline/snippet in search results; doesn't directly boost ranking but strongly affects click-through rate.
- **Header structure (H1/H2/H3)** — helps both readers and search engines parse the page's structure.
- **Internal linking** — links between a site's own pages, which spread authority and help search engines understand site structure.
- **E-E-A-T** (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) — a quality framework search engines use, especially for topics that affect health, finance, or safety.

## Common Questions

**How long does SEO take to show results?**
> Commonly months, not weeks — it depends on domain authority, competition for the keyword, and content quality. This is why SEO is usually paired with paid channels (see [SEM/PPC](sem-ppc.md)) for faster, more predictable short-term results.

**What's the difference between SEO and SEM?**
> SEO is unpaid/organic ranking; [SEM](sem-ppc.md) (Search Engine Marketing) typically refers to [paid search](sem-ppc.md) ads that appear alongside organic results.
