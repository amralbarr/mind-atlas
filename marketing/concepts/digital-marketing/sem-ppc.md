# SEM & PPC (Paid Search)

**SEM** (Search Engine Marketing) commonly refers to paid ads shown on search engine results pages. **PPC** (Pay-Per-Click) is the billing model behind most of it — the advertiser pays only when someone clicks the ad, not for the impression itself.

## How Ad Auctions Work (Simplified)

Search ad placement isn't just the highest bidder — it's usually:

> **Ad Rank = Bid × Quality Score**

Where **Quality Score** reflects expected click-through rate, ad relevance, and landing page experience. A highly relevant, well-targeted ad can outrank a higher bid with a worse Quality Score — and pay less per click for the same position.

## Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **CPC** | Cost Per Click — what's actually paid per click |
| **CPM** | Cost Per Mille (1,000 impressions) — used more for display/awareness campaigns |
| **CTR** | Click-Through Rate — clicks ÷ impressions |
| **Quality Score** | Platform's estimate of ad relevance and landing page quality |
| **Negative keywords** | Terms you explicitly exclude so ads don't show for irrelevant searches |
| **Match type** | Broad, phrase, or exact — controls how closely a search must match the keyword to trigger the ad |

## Campaign Structure

A typical account is organized: **Campaign → Ad Group → Ads/Keywords**. Campaigns usually split by budget or goal (e.g. brand vs. non-brand); ad groups within a campaign should be tightly themed so the keywords, ad copy, and landing page all match closely — a mismatch here is the most common cause of high spend with low conversion.

## SEM vs. SEO

| | SEM / PPC | SEO |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Immediate | Slow (weeks-months) |
| Cost model | Pay per click, stops when budget stops | Time/content investment, compounds over time |
| Control | Precise targeting and messaging | Indirect — depends on search engine's ranking factors |

Most mature marketing strategies run both: PPC for immediate, controllable volume; SEO for compounding, lower-marginal-cost traffic over time.

## Common Questions

**Why would a well-ranking page still run paid ads for the same keyword?**
> To capture additional real estate on the results page, defend against competitor bidding on the brand name, and because paid ads convert differently (different audience mindset, testable copy) than organic listings.

**What causes wasted PPC spend?**
> Broad match keywords without negative keywords, ad groups covering too many unrelated terms, and a landing page that doesn't match the ad's promise (hurts both Quality Score and conversion).
