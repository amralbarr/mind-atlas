# Content Marketing

Creating and distributing valuable, relevant content to attract and retain a defined audience — with the goal of eventually driving profitable action, rather than pitching a product directly in every piece.

## Content Marketing vs. Advertising

| | Content Marketing | Advertising |
|---|---|---|
| Approach | Earn attention by providing value | Buy attention directly |
| Timeline | Compounds slowly over time | Immediate, stops when spend stops |
| Trust | Builds credibility and authority | Perceived as a sales message |
| Cost model | Time/production investment | Media spend |

## Matching Content to the Journey

Content should be built for a specific [customer journey](../fundamentals/customer-journey.md) stage, not written generically:

| Stage | Content type |
|---|---|
| **Awareness** | Blog posts, educational videos, social content — broad, problem-focused |
| **Consideration** | Comparison guides, webinars, in-depth articles — solution-focused |
| **Decision** | Case studies, testimonials, product demos, free trials |
| **Retention** | Onboarding guides, newsletters, community content |

A common failure mode is producing only decision-stage content ("why choose us") when the audience is still at the awareness stage and doesn't yet know they have the problem.

## Content Formats

Blog posts, video, podcasts, infographics, whitepapers/ebooks (often gated behind an email signup for lead generation), webinars, and interactive tools (calculators, quizzes). Format choice should follow where the audience already consumes content and what the message actually needs (a process is easier to show in video; a detailed comparison is easier to reference in text).

## Content Calendar & Repurposing

A content calendar plans what publishes when, across which channels, tied to campaigns or themes. **Repurposing** — turning one piece of content into multiple formats (a webinar into a blog post, clips, and a newsletter section) — is standard practice, since production cost is high relative to distribution cost.

## Common Questions

**How is content marketing measured, if it's not directly selling?**
> Leading indicators (traffic, time on page, email signups, share of search) track engagement; lagging indicators (assisted conversions, influenced pipeline, customer retention) tie it back to revenue over a longer window than a single-touch attribution model would show.

**What's the relationship between content marketing and SEO?**
> They're mutually reinforcing: [SEO](seo-fundamentals.md) determines what content is worth creating (search demand, keyword intent) and how it should be structured to be found; content marketing is what actually gets created and distributed.
