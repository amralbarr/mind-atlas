# Conversion Rate Optimization (CRO)

The practice of systematically increasing the percentage of visitors who complete a desired action (purchase, signup, download) — usually by removing friction or doubt, rather than by driving more traffic.

## Conversion Rate

> **Conversion Rate = Conversions ÷ Visitors × 100**

CRO matters because improving conversion rate compounds with every traffic source at once — a 1% → 2% improvement doubles output from existing SEO, paid, and email traffic without spending more to acquire it.

## Where Friction and Doubt Come From

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Friction** (too hard) | Long forms, forced account creation, slow page load, confusing navigation, too many steps to checkout |
| **Doubt** (not convinced) | Unclear [value proposition](../fundamentals/value-proposition.md), no social proof, hidden pricing, no trust signals (security badges, guarantees, reviews) |

Diagnosing which one is the actual problem at a given step (usually visible in analytics as a specific drop-off point) determines the fix — adding more trust signals won't help if the real issue is a broken form.

## The CRO Process

1. **Research** — analytics (where do people drop off?), session recordings, heatmaps, user surveys.
2. **Hypothesize** — form a specific, testable belief about *why* the drop-off happens.
3. **Test** — usually via [A/B testing](ab-testing.md), changing one variable to isolate its effect.
4. **Measure & iterate** — implement the winner, move to the next highest-impact hypothesis.

## Common Levers

- **Headline / [value proposition](../fundamentals/value-proposition.md) clarity** — often the single highest-leverage change on a landing page.
- **Call-to-action ([CTA](../digital-marketing/call-to-action-cta.md))** — wording, placement, visual prominence, and reducing the number of competing CTAs on a page.
- **Social proof** — testimonials, logos, review counts, case studies.
- **Form length** — every additional field tends to reduce completion rate; ask only for what's truly needed at that step.
- **Page speed** — slow load time increases abandonment, especially on mobile.

## Common Questions

**Is a "good" conversion rate a useful benchmark?**
> Only loosely — conversion rate varies enormously by industry, traffic source quality, and funnel stage. A business's own trend over time (and against its own traffic-source mix) is a far more reliable signal than an external benchmark.

**How does CRO relate to CAC?**
> Improving conversion rate directly lowers [CAC](marketing-kpis-metrics.md) — the same ad spend produces more customers, without any change to targeting or bids.
