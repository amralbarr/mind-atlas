# Delivery Rate & Return Rate

For any business shipping physical products, the delivery success rate (the inverse of the return/rejection rate) is one of the most underrated metrics — it can silently double the real cost of every sale, even while the marketing dashboard looks healthy.

## Why It's Easy to Miss

Ad platforms report clicks, messages, and leads — not what happened after the courier left the warehouse. A campaign can show a great cost-per-lead and a great [CAC](marketing-kpis-metrics.md) on paper while half of "sold" orders bounce back as failed deliveries or returns, a fact that never shows up in the ads dashboard at all. This is why delivery/return rate has to be tracked separately, alongside — not instead of — advertising metrics.

## The Real Cost of a Low Delivery Rate

> If 10,000 units are marketed and only 5,000 actually deliver, the true cost-per-delivered-unit is roughly double the cost-per-lead the ad dashboard reports.

A business comparing two ad agencies purely on cost-per-message or cost-per-lead can end up choosing the *worse* option — an agency generating cheaper but lower-quality leads that convert to far fewer successful deliveries can cost more overall than a pricier agency whose leads deliver at a much higher rate.

## Choosing a Shipping/Fulfillment Partner

The same logic applies to selecting a courier or 3PL (third-party logistics) provider — the decision shouldn't be made on price-per-shipment alone. A cheaper courier with a materially worse delivery/return rate can be more expensive overall once the cost of undelivered inventory, refunds, and repeat shipping attempts is counted.

## The Two Metrics to Watch Together

| Metric | What it reveals |
|---|---|
| **Delivery rate** | % of orders that successfully reach and are accepted by the customer |
| **Return/rejection rate** | % of orders that come back — due to the customer refusing, being unreachable, or a fulfillment error |

A sudden shift in either — even while lead volume and ad cost look stable — is a leading indicator that something changed either in lead quality (targeting drifted toward less-committed buyers) or in fulfillment operations (a courier's service degraded), and should be investigated immediately rather than only when overall revenue visibly drops.

## Common Questions

**How does this connect to MER?**
> [MER](marketing-efficiency-ratio-mer.md) as commonly discussed accounts for ad and production spend against total revenue, but a business with a high return rate should track *net* revenue (after returns) against that same marketing cost — a rising return rate quietly worsens MER even if gross bookings look unchanged.

**Should marketing be blamed for a rising return rate?**
> Not automatically — a rising return rate can come from a change in targeting (attracting less-qualified leads), a fulfillment/courier problem, or a product/expectation mismatch. Diagnosing which one it is requires looking at lead quality metrics, courier performance, and customer feedback together, not assuming the cause without checking.
