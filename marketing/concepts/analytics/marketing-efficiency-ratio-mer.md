# Marketing Efficiency Ratio (MER) vs. ROAS

**ROAS** (Return on Ad Spend) divides revenue by *ad spend alone*. **MER** (Marketing Efficiency Ratio) divides revenue by *total marketing cost* — ad spend plus everything spent to produce the marketing (content production, creator/influencer fees, PR, photography/video). MER is the more honest number because it reflects what marketing actually cost the business, not just the media bill.

## The Formula

> **MER = Total Revenue ÷ (Ad Spend + Content Production + Creator/Influencer Fees + Other Marketing Production Costs)**

## Why ROAS Alone Is Misleading

A business can look at a "5x ROAS" on ad spend and feel great, while ignoring that 200,000-300,000 in content production (video shoots, creators, UGC) was spent to produce the assets those ads ran — money that came out of the same marketing budget but never appears in the ROAS calculation. Once that's included, the *real* efficiency ratio might be 3.5x, not 5x. Neither number is "wrong" — they answer different questions — but treating ROAS as the full picture hides the true cost of growth.

## Worked Example

| | Ad spend only (ROAS) | Full marketing cost (MER) |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 1,000,000 | 1,000,000 |
| Ad spend | 200,000 | 200,000 |
| Content production / creators | — (excluded) | 85,000 |
| **Ratio** | **5.0x** | **3.5x** |

The business is still profitable at 3.5x — the point isn't that MER is "worse," it's that MER is the number that should actually feed pricing, budget, and profitability decisions, because it reflects the real cost structure.

## When to Use Which

- **ROAS** — useful for comparing *ad creative and targeting performance* against itself over time, isolated from production cost swings.
- **MER** — the number to report up, to price against, and to make scale/cut decisions with, since it reflects total marketing investment against total return.

## Common Questions

**Does a lower MER always mean marketing is failing?**
> No — a business deliberately investing heavily in high-production content or influencer/PR campaigns to build a new brand (see [Marketing Budget Allocation](../growth/marketing-budget-allocation.md)) will show a lower MER short-term by design; the investment is expected to pay back in brand equity and reduced acquisition cost over time.

**How does MER relate to CAC?**
> [CAC](marketing-kpis-metrics.md) is cost per customer; MER is a ratio of total revenue to total marketing cost across the whole funnel. A rising CAC is often the first symptom that will eventually show up as a falling MER — CAC is the earlier, more granular warning signal.
