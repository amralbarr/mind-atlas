# Brand Identity

The collection of visual, verbal, and experiential elements a company controls to shape how it's perceived — distinct from **brand image** (how it's *actually* perceived, which the company doesn't fully control) and **[positioning](../fundamentals/positioning.md)** (the strategic decision the identity is built to reinforce).

## Core Elements

| Element | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Visual identity** | Logo, color palette, typography, imagery style |
| **Verbal identity** | Name, tagline, tone of voice — see [Brand Voice & Messaging](brand-voice-messaging.md) |
| **Experiential identity** | Packaging, in-app experience, customer service tone, physical spaces |

## Brand Identity vs. Brand Image vs. Positioning

| Term | Who controls it | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **Brand identity** | The company | What the company designs and puts out into the world |
| **Brand image** | The customer's perception | What actually forms in the customer's mind — may or may not match the identity |
| **[Positioning](../fundamentals/positioning.md)** | The company (strategy) | The deliberate strategic target for what the brand image *should* be |

A gap between identity and image is a sign the messaging, product experience, or targeting isn't landing as intended.

## Why Consistency Matters

A brand is built through repeated exposure — the same logo, tone, and promise showing up consistently across every touchpoint (ads, website, support, packaging) is what makes a brand recognizable and trustworthy. Inconsistency (different tone on social vs. sales calls, a redesign that abandons prior visual equity) resets that recognition.

## Brand Equity

The accumulated value a brand has earned from consistent, positive experiences — it shows up as price premiums customers are willing to pay, faster trust with new products under the same name, and resilience during a crisis or bad press.

## Common Questions

**What's the difference between a logo and a brand?**
> A logo is one visual asset within the identity; the brand is the entire perception built over time — including reputation, product quality, and every customer interaction. Redesigning a logo doesn't change the brand.

**Should a startup invest heavily in brand identity early?**
> Usually a lean, consistent identity (clear name, simple logo, one clear tone of voice) is enough early on. Heavy brand investment tends to pay off once [positioning](../fundamentals/positioning.md) is validated and repeat exposure at scale actually matters.
