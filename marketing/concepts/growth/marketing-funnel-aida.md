# Marketing Funnel & AIDA

The **funnel** is the standard way to visualize how a large pool of prospects narrows down to a small number of customers, stage by stage. **AIDA** (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action) is one of the oldest funnel models, originally built for advertising copy, and still a useful shorthand for structuring a single piece of marketing content.

## The Funnel Shape

```
   Awareness      (widest — everyone who could hear about you)
      ↓
   Interest
      ↓
   Consideration
      ↓
   Intent
      ↓
   Purchase       (narrowest — actual customers)
```

Each stage loses some percentage of people (drop-off) — the funnel is "leaky" by design, and CRO/marketing work is largely about improving the conversion rate *between* specific stages, not the whole funnel at once. See [Customer Journey](../fundamentals/customer-journey.md) for the same idea framed around the customer's experience rather than the business's stages.

## AIDA (for a single piece of content or an ad)

| Stage | Goal | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Attention** | Interrupt the scroll/stop the eye | A bold headline or striking visual |
| **Interest** | Make it relevant to them | Naming their specific problem |
| **Desire** | Make them want the outcome | Showing the benefit, social proof |
| **Action** | Tell them exactly what to do next | A clear, single CTA |

AIDA works at the scale of one ad or landing page; the funnel works at the scale of the whole customer base over time. Many teams use both — AIDA to write a specific asset, the funnel to see where that asset sits in the bigger picture.

## AARRR ("Pirate Metrics")

A newer funnel model built for digital/product-led growth, tracking the metrics that matter after acquisition too:

| Stage | Question |
|---|---|
| **Acquisition** | How do users find you? |
| **Activation** | Do they have a good first experience? |
| **Retention** | Do they come back? |
| **Referral** | Do they tell others? |
| **Revenue** | Do they pay? |

Unlike AIDA (pre-purchase only), AARRR explicitly tracks what happens *after* the sale — a reminder that acquisition-only thinking misses retention and referral, which are often cheaper growth levers than new acquisition.

## Common Questions

**Where does the funnel break down as a model?**
> Real journeys aren't strictly linear — people re-enter at different stages, skip stages via referral, or run parallel research across many channels before converting. The funnel is a planning and measurement tool, not a literal description of behavior.

**How does the funnel relate to growth hacking?**
> [Growth hacking](growth-hacking-basics.md) is the practice of finding fast, often unconventional ways to improve the conversion rate *at a specific funnel stage*, frequently the acquisition or activation stage.
