# The Marketing Mix (4Ps)

The classic framework for the controllable variables a business uses to market a product: **Product, Price, Place, Promotion**. Every marketing decision fits into one of these levers.

## The Four Elements

| Element | Question it answers | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Product** | What are you selling, and why does it matter to the customer? | Features, quality, design, variants, packaging, branding |
| **Price** | What does it cost, and how is that justified? | List price, discounts, payment terms, perceived value |
| **Place** | Where and how does the customer get it? | Distribution channels, online vs. retail, logistics, availability |
| **Promotion** | How do people find out about it and get persuaded to buy? | Advertising, PR, content, sales promotions, social media |

## Why It Matters

The 4Ps force a decision to be evaluated from all four angles at once — a great product with the wrong price, poor distribution, or no promotion still fails. Consistency across all four is what makes a marketing strategy coherent rather than a set of disconnected tactics.

## Extensions

Later frameworks extend this for services and modern marketing:

- **7Ps** adds **People**, **Process**, **Physical Evidence** — common in service industries where the experience itself is part of the product.
- **4Cs** (Lauterborn) reframes the 4Ps from the customer's perspective: Product → **Customer needs**, Price → **Cost to the customer**, Place → **Convenience**, Promotion → **Communication**.

## Common Questions

**Is the 4Ps model still relevant in digital marketing?**
> Yes, but "Place" now includes digital channels (marketplaces, app stores, direct-to-consumer sites) and "Promotion" includes SEO, paid social, and influencer marketing alongside traditional advertising.

**How does the 4Ps model relate to positioning?**
> The 4Ps are the levers; [positioning](positioning.md) is the strategic decision about how those levers combine to occupy a specific place in the customer's mind relative to competitors.
