# Customer Journey

The full path a person takes from first becoming aware of a problem or product to becoming a customer — and beyond, into loyalty or advocacy. Mapping it reveals where marketing needs to show up and what message fits each moment.

## The Common Stages

| Stage | Customer's mindset | Marketing's job |
|---|---|---|
| **Awareness** | "I have a problem" | Get discovered — SEO, ads, social, PR |
| **Consideration** | "What are my options?" | Educate and differentiate — content, comparisons, reviews |
| **Decision** | "Which one do I choose?" | Remove doubt — case studies, demos, pricing clarity, trials |
| **Purchase** | "I'm buying" | Frictionless checkout/signup |
| **Retention** | "Am I getting value?" | Onboarding, support, ongoing communication |
| **Advocacy** | "I'd recommend this" | Referral programs, reviews, community |

This maps closely onto the classic **AIDA** model (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action) and the **marketing funnel** — see [Marketing Funnel & AIDA](../growth/marketing-funnel-aida.md) for the funnel view of the same idea.

## Touchpoints

A touchpoint is any point of contact between the customer and the brand during the journey — a search result, an ad, a support email, a checkout page. Mapping touchpoints against stages is what a **customer journey map** actually is: a grid of (stage × channel × message × emotion).

## Why It Matters

- Prevents marketing from over-investing in one stage (e.g. all awareness spend, no retention effort) while a different stage is actually leaking customers.
- Clarifies which content or channel belongs where — a detailed comparison guide is a consideration-stage asset, not an awareness-stage one.
- Makes conversion problems diagnosable: if traffic is high but signups are low, the leak is likely at consideration or decision, not awareness.

## A Real Funnel, Built Stage by Stage

A documented example: launching an entirely new, unproven service with no paid-social track record, on a near-zero budget. Stage one was a ~3-minute awareness video addressing the underlying problem (not the service itself) — long enough that only genuinely interested viewers watched a meaningful portion. That video ran broadly for about a week. Stage two retargeted specifically the people who'd watched at least 25% of it with a second, deeper video. Stage three retargeted that same narrowing group again with further content. The first paying customer arrived on day 29 — a reminder that even a well-built funnel can take weeks to produce its first result, and that judging it too early (before the sequence has run its course) would have looked like failure. See [Video-View-Percentage Retargeting](../digital-marketing/video-view-retargeting.md) for the mechanics of how the retargeting stages were built.

## Common Questions

**Is the customer journey linear?**
> Rarely in practice — people loop back (re-research after a decision, churn and return), skip stages (a strong referral can skip straight to decision), or run journeys in parallel across devices. The stages are a model for organizing marketing effort, not a guarantee of real user behavior.

**How does this relate to [conversion rate optimization](../analytics/conversion-rate-optimization.md)?**
> [CRO](../analytics/conversion-rate-optimization.md) is the practice of improving the conversion rate *at* a specific journey stage — usually decision → purchase — by removing friction or doubt at that step.
