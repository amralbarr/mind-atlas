# Buyer Personas & Target Audience

A **buyer persona** is a semi-fictional profile of an ideal customer, built from real data and research, used to guide marketing, product, and content decisions. **Target audience** is the broader group a campaign is aimed at; a persona makes that group concrete enough to write for.

## What a Persona Typically Includes

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Name & role | Makes the persona feel like a real person, not an abstraction |
| Demographics | Age, income, location, job title |
| Goals | What they're trying to achieve |
| Pain points | What's blocking them today |
| Buying triggers | What makes them start looking for a solution |
| Objections | What makes them hesitate to buy |
| Preferred channels | Where they consume information (LinkedIn, email, search, etc.) |

## Example

> **"Efficiency Emad"** — Operations manager at a 50-person company. Goal: reduce manual reporting work. Pain point: spends 5+ hours/week on spreadsheets. Trigger: a missed deadline caused by a reporting error. Objection: "Will my team actually adopt a new tool?" Channel: LinkedIn, industry newsletters.

## Why Personas Matter

- They force marketing and product teams to write for a specific person instead of "everyone."
- They surface the actual objections a sales or landing page needs to address.
- They keep messaging consistent across a team — everyone is writing for the same imagined reader.

## B2B vs. B2C Personas

- **B2B** personas usually center on a *role* (e.g. "IT Director") because the buyer is rarely the only decision-maker — see also the buying committee/multiple stakeholders.
- **B2C** personas center on an individual's lifestyle, values, and personal motivations.

## Common Questions

**How many personas should a business have?**
> As few as necessary to cover meaningfully different buying motivations — often 2-4. More than that and messaging becomes hard to maintain.

**How is a persona different from a market segment?**
> A [market segment](market-segmentation.md) is a data-defined group (e.g. "urban professionals 25-40"); a persona is a narrative, human representation of a typical member of that segment, used to make writing and design decisions easier.
