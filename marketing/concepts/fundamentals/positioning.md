# Positioning

The deliberate act of designing how a product is perceived in the mind of a target customer, *relative to competitors*. Positioning isn't what you do to the product — it's what you do to the customer's perception of it.

## Positioning vs. Value Proposition vs. Branding

| Concept | Focus |
|---|---|
| [Value proposition](value-proposition.md) | The specific claim of benefit + differentiation |
| **Positioning** | The strategic space you claim relative to competitors, in the customer's mind |
| [Branding](../branding/brand-identity.md) | The identity, personality, and feel that reinforces the position over time |

A value proposition is usually the written articulation of a positioning strategy; branding is what makes that position memorable and trusted.

## Common Positioning Strategies

- **By attribute/benefit** — "The fastest," "The safest" (Volvo → safety)
- **By price/quality** — premium vs. value (Rolex vs. Casio)
- **By use case** — "For remote teams," "For solo founders"
- **Against a competitor** — "Unlike [X], we..."
- **Against the category** — positioning as something entirely new, not a variant of an existing category

## Positioning Statement Template

> For **[target segment]**, **[product]** is the **[category]** that **[key differentiating benefit]**, because **[reason to believe]**.

## A Positioning Map

A simple way to visualize competitive positioning: plot competitors on two axes that matter to customers (e.g. Price vs. Quality, or Simplicity vs. Power), and find the open space your product can credibly own. If every competitor clusters in the same corner, that's often a positioning opportunity — or a sign that corner is a genuine dead end. Validate with buyers before betting on it.

## Common Questions

**Can positioning change over time?**
> Yes — repositioning happens when a market shifts, when data shows the current position isn't resonating, or when a company enters a new segment. It's expensive to change once established, since it requires shifting an existing perception.

**What's the difference between market segmentation and positioning?**
> [Segmentation](market-segmentation.md) decides *who* to target; positioning decides *how* the product should be perceived by that chosen audience relative to alternatives.
