# In-House Marketing Team vs. Agency

One of the most common, expensive mistakes a growing business makes: building an in-house marketing team specifically *to save money* on an agency, without appointing anyone to actually lead and direct that team.

## The Failure Pattern

A business paying an agency and seeing results decides the agency's fee is avoidable — "let's just hire our own media buyer, content writer, and designer in-house." The team gets hired. Nobody is put in charge of setting strategy, prioritizing work, or being accountable for results — because the entire point of the move was cost-cutting, and a strategy-setting hire (a marketing manager/director) felt like an extra cost to avoid. The in-house team executes tasks without direction, results degrade, turnover rises as morale drops, and the business ends up worse off than when it was paying the agency.

## The Missing Role Is Always the Same

Whichever model a business chooses — self-managed, agency, or in-house team — it needs exactly one thing to work: someone internal who understands marketing well enough to set direction and evaluate results. Three configurations, one non-negotiable requirement:

| Model | Who still needs to exist internally |
|---|---|
| **Founder/owner executes marketing personally** | Fine only while ad spend is small enough that the owner can realistically manage it alongside running the business |
| **Agency handles execution** | An internal marketing manager who can brief the agency, evaluate their reporting, and hold them accountable — see [Client Expectation Management](../client-relationships/client-expectation-management.md) for the agency side of this relationship |
| **In-house team handles execution** | A marketing manager/director leading that team — without this, the team is a cost center with no direction |

## A Rough Spend-Based Threshold

As daily/monthly ad spend grows, the case for dedicated internal marketing leadership strengthens:

- **Small daily spend** — an owner or a single dedicated marketer can often manage this directly.
- **Moderate daily spend** — a dedicated internal media buyer or marketing manager becomes necessary; the owner can no longer realistically also run the day-to-day dashboard.
- **Large daily/monthly spend** — the case shifts to either a well-managed agency relationship *or* a properly-led in-house team; ad-hoc management at this scale reliably wastes significant money.

The exact thresholds vary by business and category — the point is the *direction* of the relationship: more spend increasingly requires more dedicated, accountable oversight, not less.

## Sourcing a Marketing Manager the Right Way

Hiring a marketing manager through a generic job posting is high-risk, because a candidate who is simply articulate and well-spoken can pass an interview with someone who isn't equipped to evaluate marketing expertise — resulting in a confident-sounding hire with no real track record. A more reliable path: get a referral through a trusted professional network (an already-successful peer's team, a respected agency that can recommend someone), which pre-filters for actual demonstrated results rather than interview performance alone.

## Common Questions

**If a company already has a good agency relationship, is building an in-house team still worth pursuing?**
> Usually only once volume/spend justifies dedicated headcount *and* someone is ready to lead that team — building an in-house team as a lateral move (same spend, just insourced) without solving the leadership gap tends to reproduce the failure pattern above rather than saving money.

**Is an academic marketing background (an MBA, formal marketing courses) enough to qualify someone as a marketing manager?**
> Credentials alone don't substitute for demonstrated hands-on experience and results in the specific category — a candidate should be evaluated on a real track record, not coursework alone, especially for a role that will make real budget-allocation decisions.
